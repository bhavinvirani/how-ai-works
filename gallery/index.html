This is not how it worked originally

Early systems split on spaces and punctuation. That falls apart on languages that do not put spaces between words, which is a large share of them.

Easy to get backwards

More tokens does not mean more meaning. A long word split into six pieces carries the same idea it always did — it just costs more to process.

Checkpoint

Why would a model split an unfamiliar word into pieces rather than treat it as one new word?

Show answerHide answer

Because its vocabulary is fixed and finite. A word it has never seen has no entry to look up — but the pieces do, so it can build a meaning out of parts it already knows.
