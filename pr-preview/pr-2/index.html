How AI Actually Works

A site that explains how these systems really work, without assuming you have a maths degree or a machine learning job.

Most explanations of AI pick one of two bad options. They either wave their hands and tell you it’s “like a brain,” which explains nothing, or they open with a wall of notation that assumes you already know the answer.

This one takes a third route. Every idea starts with a problem you already recognise from ordinary life. The name for the idea comes after you already understand it. Then you get a diagram, and then you get an instrument you can actually adjust and break, because reading about a thing and playing with it are not the same kind of knowing.

What a formula looks like here

Formulas appear only when they make an idea clearer than words alone would, and every one of them is followed by a plain-English reading.

Here is the measure of how similar two things are, which comes up once we get to how models compare pieces of text:

similarity(A,B)=A⋅B∥A∥ ∥B∥\text{similarity}(A, B) = \frac{A \cdot B}{\|A\| \, \|B\|}similarity(A,B)=∥A∥∥B∥A⋅B​

In words: point two arrows out from the same spot and ask how closely they aim the same way — ignoring entirely how long either arrow happens to be.

That last clause is the whole point of the formula, and it is the part that gets lost when people only show you the symbols.
