The shape of the whole thing

60 topics sounds like 60 things to learn. It is not. Nearly every unit here exists because of the one before it: something breaks, the next idea is the repair, and that repair creates the next problem. This page is that sequence, drawn once and then written out.

The units are grouped into the Parts of the curriculum. On the map each Part is a box with its name across the top, and the units inside it are what you would read in that stretch — so a Part is a place on the map, not a colour. An arrow runs from a unit to the one that needs it first. It does not mean "these two are related"; it means "read this one before that one".

The interesting bits are where the single line splits. A box with several arrows leaving it is an idea that more than one later unit is built on, and those are the ones worth slowing down for. The numbers are generated from the arrows: any order that never puts a unit ahead of something it depends on would work, and this is one of them.

2 How a machine learns

  1. 5
    How the dials turn themselves

    Learning is one small cycle — guess, compare, blame, nudge — run a few million times. No single pass is worth anything; the pile of them is worth everything.

    Read first: The machine made of dials

    Related: The table underneath everything, Why rules run out

  2. 6
    The number it chases

    A machine can only ever improve against one number, so whatever you choose to count is exactly what it becomes good at, and everything else is free to get worse.

    Read first: How the dials turn themselves

    Related: The machine made of dials, The table underneath everything, Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose

  3. 7
    Feeling your way downhill

    A machine never sees where the good settings are. It feels which way the ground tips under its feet, steps that way, and repeats, which makes training a search rather than a calculation.

    Read first: The number it chases

    Related: Whose fault it was, and how much, How the dials turn themselves, The machine made of dials

  4. 8
    How far to step

    Knowing which way is downhill says nothing about how far to walk, and that one distance, chosen by a person before training starts, decides whether the machine settles, crawls, or destroys itself.

    Read first: Feeling your way downhill

    Related: How the dials turn themselves, The machine made of dials

3 Where the answers come from

  1. 9
    When somebody already knows the answer

    When every example arrives with the right answer written beside it, the machine can be corrected exactly, and the number of answers somebody was willing to write down is the ceiling on what you can build.

    Read first: How the dials turn themselves

    Related: The table underneath everything, The number it chases

  2. 10
    The groups it invents

    Take the answer column away and a machine can still find structure — it gathers together the rows that resemble each other. But the groups are its own invention, and deciding what they mean is left to you.

    Read first: The table underneath everything

    Related: The map nobody drew, The number it chases, The machine made of dials

  3. 11
    Nobody says what was right

    With no correct answer to compare against, a learner acts, waits for a number, and does more of whatever earned one, which makes it superb at raising the number and entirely indifferent to what you meant by it.

    Read first: The number it chases

    Related: How the dials turn themselves, Why rules run out, Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose, The only way to try harder is to write more

  4. 12
    The answers were in the text all along

    Cover up part of something a person already wrote and ask a machine to put it back — the question and its marked answer were both in the text already, which is what made training on everything ever written possible.

    Read first: When somebody already knows the answer, The table underneath everything

    Related: The number it chases, How the dials turn themselves, It read everything once, and then stopped, The only question it ever gets asked

4 When a good score lies

  1. 13
    The perfect score that proves nothing

    A machine with enough freedom will memorise its examples instead of learning from them — scoring perfectly on everything it studied, and falling apart on the first case it has not already seen.

    Read first: How the dials turn themselves

    Related: The machine made of dials, The number it chases

  2. 14
    The pile nobody is allowed to look at

    A machine can only be judged on examples it has never seen, so a slice of the data is hidden before anything starts, and a third slice exists because you, not the machine, are what spoils the second.

    Read first: The perfect score that proves nothing, The table underneath everything

    Related: Why rules run out, The number it chases, The three you broke fixing one

  3. 15
    Two ways to be wrong

    When the answer you are hunting for is rare, a machine that says no to everybody scores ninety-nine per cent, so the only honest way to judge one is to count its two kinds of mistake separately, and decide by hand which you would rather have.

    Read first: The number it chases

    Related: Multiply, add, and bend, The table underneath everything, Why rules run out, The three you broke fixing one

5 What the machine is made of

  1. 16
    Multiply, add, and bend

    A neuron multiplies each fact it is handed by its own dial, adds the results up, and bends the total, and without that last small step, a whole stack of them collapses into something one of them could already do.

    Read first: The machine made of dials

    Related: The billions are in the wiring

  2. 17
    The billions are in the wiring

    Neurons standing side by side make a layer, and every line between one layer and the next is a single adjustable number, which is why joining a thousand neurons to a thousand costs a million of them.

    Read first: Multiply, add, and bend

    Related: The jobs nobody handed out, The machine that cannot try harder

  3. 18
    The machine that cannot try harder

    A finished model answers by running the numbers through its layers once, in order — identical arithmetic whether the question is trivial or baffling, and no route back to think again.

    Read first: The billions are in the wiring

    Related: Whose fault it was, and how much, The jobs nobody handed out, The only way to try harder is to write more

  4. 19
    Whose fault it was, and how much

    One sweep backwards through the layers works out how much of a wrong answer each dial is responsible for, and it costs about what the forward run that produced the answer cost.

    Read first: Feeling your way downhill, The machine that cannot try harder

    Related: The good idea that had to wait

  5. 20
    The jobs nobody handed out

    Nobody tells a network to look for edges, or ears, or which person a "she" belongs to — the layers sort themselves into a ladder from crude fragments up to whole meanings, because that arrangement is what makes the wrongness score smallest.

    Read first: The billions are in the wiring

    Related: The good idea that had to wait, The map nobody drew

  6. 21
    The good idea that had to wait

    Stacking layers deep is a 1980s idea that sat unusable for twenty years until data, graphics cards and a few unglamorous fixes turned up, and what depth buys, once it works, is reuse, because a part built once can be shared by everything above it.

    Read first: The jobs nobody handed out, Whose fault it was, and how much

    Related: The map nobody drew

  7. 22
    The map nobody drew

    Give every word a position rather than a name, learn those positions from the company each word keeps, and distance starts to mean similarity — with whole directions across the space turning out to mean something too.

    Read first: The answers were in the text all along

    Related: The word that has not arrived yet, Nearest is not the same as matching

6 Why language broke everything

  1. 23
    The word that has not arrived yet

    Machines had photographs solved years before they had sentences, because what a word means can depend on where it sits, on a word a long way off, and sometimes on a word that has not been read yet.

    Read first: The map nobody drew

    Related: Nobody asks you to spell Smith, Every word asks the whole sentence at once

  2. 24
    Nobody asks you to spell Smith

    Text is cut into chunks from a fixed list of about a hundred thousand before a model sees any of it, not letters and not words, which is why the bill is counted in chunks, why a machine that writes essays cannot count the letters in a word, and why the same sentence costs several times more in some languages than in English.

    Read first: The map nobody drew

    Related: One word at a time, and the beginning is gone, It has never remembered a word you said

  3. 25
    One word at a time, and the beginning is gone

    Machines used to read a sentence one word at a time into a memory that never got any bigger, which loses the start of anything long and, worse, leaves almost every processor you own with nothing to do.

    Read first: The word that has not arrived yet

    Related: Every word asks the whole sentence at once

7 The idea that cracked it

  1. 26
    Every word asks the whole sentence at once

    Instead of passing one fading summary along a line, every word puts a question to every other word at the same moment and rebuilds its own meaning out of whichever ones answered.

    Read first: One word at a time, and the beginning is gone

    Related: The jobs nobody handed out, The same block, ninety-six times

  2. 27
    The jobs nobody handed out

    One round of attention can only follow one kind of relationship, so a model runs many of them at the same moment, and what each one ends up watching is settled by training rather than by anyone's decision.

    Read first: Every word asks the whole sentence at once

    Related: The same block, ninety-six times

  3. 28
    Same words, any order, same answer

    Attention compares every word with every other and never once asks which came first, so the order has to be stamped into the words themselves before any comparing starts.

    Read first: Every word asks the whole sentence at once

    Related: The same block, ninety-six times, It has never remembered a word you said

  4. 29
    The same block, ninety-six times

    Attention on its own will not stack, so the transformer wraps it in a shortcut, a rescale and a step where each word thinks alone, and that one block, repeated a fixed number of times, is what nearly every modern model is built from.

    Read first: Every word asks the whole sentence at once, Same words, any order, same answer

    Related: The only question it ever gets asked

  5. 30
    Nothing in it ever learned to see

    Cut a picture into squares, turn each square into the same kind of number-list a word piece becomes, and the machine from the last four units reads it unchanged, which is also why it can describe a photograph fluently and still miscount what is in it.

    Read first: The map nobody drew, The same block, ninety-six times, Nobody asks you to spell Smith

8 What a large model does

  1. 31
    The only question it ever gets asked

    A large language model is the transformer from the last Part built very large and tuned on a vast amount of text, and the only thing it does is put a number on every entry in its vocabulary saying how well that entry would come next.

    Read first: The same block, ninety-six times

    Related: Every word is final the moment it is written, Nothing in there ever checked

  2. 32
    Every word is final the moment it is written

    A model writes one piece, glues it onto the end of what it has already written, and reads the whole thing back before writing the next, which is why it never had a plan, and why nothing it has written can be taken back.

    Read first: The only question it ever gets asked

    Related: The dial that cannot make it cleverer, The steps are not a report. They are the work

  3. 33
    The dial that cannot make it cleverer

    The model hands over a row of percentages, and one dial decides how sharply those percentages are treated before a piece is drawn from them — stretching or squashing the gaps, and changing nothing whatever about what the model rated best.

    Read first: Every word is final the moment it is written

    Related: Nothing in there ever checked, The three you broke fixing one

  4. 34
    It has never remembered a word you said

    A model keeps nothing between messages, so the whole conversation is sent again from the top every turn, and all of it, plus room for the reply, has to fit inside one window whose size never changes.

    Read first: Every word asks the whole sentence at once

    Related: The skill is what you leave out, The model is the easy part

  5. 35
    Knowing how good it would be before building it

    Quality improved so smoothly with size, data and computing that it could be forecast before the machine existed — while some abilities looked as though they switched on all at once, which is either a real threshold or an artefact of all-or-nothing scoring, and is still argued about.

    Read first: The only question it ever gets asked

    Related: It read everything once, and then stopped, The expensive way to do something easy

9 Turning a model into an assistant

  1. 36
    It read everything once, and then stopped

    Everything a model knows arrives in one enormous, one-off pass over a pile of text that was filtered far harder than anyone expects, and when the pass ends, what it knows stops there and never moves again.

    Read first: The answers were in the text all along

    Related: Answering was never part of the deal, Train the habit, hand over the fact

  2. 37
    Answering was never part of the deal

    A freshly pretrained model can do exactly one thing, continue whatever text is in front of it, so a question typed at it gets continued rather than answered, and the knowledge it would have needed was in there the whole time.

    Read first: It read everything once, and then stopped

    Related: Train the habit, hand over the fact, Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose

  3. 38
    Train the habit, hand over the fact

    A second short training run over a few thousand hand-picked examples is the right way to change how a model behaves and a poor, expensive way to install what it needs to know, because training moves dials, and moving a dial files nothing anywhere you can look up, correct or quote.

    Read first: Answering was never part of the deal

    Related: Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose, The model is the easy part

  4. 39
    Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose

    There is no way to write down what a good answer is, so people are shown two and asked which is better, and a second model trained to predict those picks becomes the number the first model can finally be trained against.

    Read first: Nobody says what was right, The number it chases

    Related: The only way to try harder is to write more

10 Why it behaves like that

  1. 40
    The only way to try harder is to write more

    A finished model has no dial for effort — one question, one fixed sweep — so the only way to spend more computation on a hard problem is to produce more text, which is why the models sold as reasoning models are trained to write their working out before they answer.

    Read first: Every word is final the moment it is written, Nobody says what was right

    Related: The steps are not a report. They are the work

  2. 41
    Nothing in there ever checked

    No step anywhere in a language model consults a fact, so an answer it invented comes out of the same arithmetic, at the same cost, as one that is true, and arrives written just as well.

    Read first: The only question it ever gets asked, Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose

    Related: The model is the easy part

  3. 42
    The refusal is written by the same hand

    Declining is not a separate checker standing in front of the model. It is the same dials, nudged by the same preference training as everything else, which is why refusals are inconsistent, why harmless questions that merely resemble harmful ones get caught, and why the boundary is a slope rather than a rule.

    Read first: Nobody could write it, but anyone could choose

    Related: There are no magic words, only narrower openings, Anything it reads can give it orders

11 Asking well

  1. 43
    There are no magic words, only narrower openings

    Your prompt is not a request but the opening of a document the model has to finish, so everything that reliably works — naming the reader, naming the shape, naming the purpose — works by cutting down what could plausibly come next.

    Read first: Every word is final the moment it is written

    Related: The example you would not have written down, The skill is what you leave out

  2. 44
    The example you would not have written down

    A few finished examples pin the format and the house vocabulary far harder than any description of them, and the one worth the most is the awkward case nobody thinks to write down — the ambiguous request, the empty field, the thing that should be handed back rather than answered.

    Read first: There are no magic words, only narrower openings

    Related: Train the habit, hand over the fact

  3. 45
    The steps are not a report. They are the work

    Asking a model to write its working out buys it a fresh pass through the machine for every piece it writes, and leaves each finished step sitting in the text where every later step can read it.

    Read first: The only way to try harder is to write more, Every word is final the moment it is written

12 Giving it your own documents

  1. 46
    The model is the easy part

    Look the answer up in your own documents first and hand the passages over with the question, and the model will answer from those, which puts the ceiling on every answer at the quality of the search rather than the quality of the model.

    Read first: Nothing in there ever checked

    Related: The answer is in there, and nothing can find it, Nearest is not the same as matching

  2. 47
    The answer is in there, and nothing can find it

    A document has to be cut into passages before anything can search it, and a passage that never says what it is about is invisible — no matter how good the model reading it afterwards.

    Read first: The model is the easy part, The map nobody drew

    Related: Nearest is not the same as matching

  3. 48
    Nearest is not the same as matching

    A question is turned into a position and the passages nearest it come back, which finds the right page even when it shares not one word with the question, and misses an exact code that plain word matching finds instantly, which is why real systems run both.

    Read first: The map nobody drew, The model is the easy part, The answer is in there, and nothing can find it

  4. 49
    The skill is what you leave out

    The standing instructions, the tool descriptions, the conversation, the fetched passages and the room for the answer are all spending from one fixed window, so the real work is allocation, and a bigger window moves the moment you run out rather than removing it.

    Read first: It has never remembered a word you said, The model is the easy part

    Related: The step nobody wrote down

13 Letting it act

  1. 50
    Nothing it writes ever runs itself

    A model cannot run anything. It can only produce text asking for a function to be called, and every decision about whether to run it, with what arguments and with whose credentials, is made by the code that receives the request.

    Read first: Nothing in there ever checked

    Related: Fifty-four ways into nine buildings, The step nobody wrote down

  2. 51
    Fifty-four ways into nine buildings

    Connecting six applications to nine systems by hand takes fifty-four separate pieces of plumbing; one agreed way for a tool to describe itself and be called makes it fifteen, each written once.

    Read first: Nothing it writes ever runs itself

    Related: Anything it reads can give it orders, The step nobody wrote down

  3. 52
    The step nobody wrote down

    An agent is the tool-use loop with one thing changed, the model picks the next step instead of following a sequence you wrote, and both what it can suddenly do and everything that goes wrong with it come out of that single move.

    Read first: Nothing it writes ever runs itself, The skill is what you leave out

    Related: The three you broke fixing one

14 Knowing whether it works

  1. 53
    The three you broke fixing one

    Nothing about an AI system can be judged from the one case in front of you, so a change is an improvement only if a fixed set of real cases with known-good answers says it is, and almost nobody has one.

    Read first: The pile nobody is allowed to look at, Two ways to be wrong, The step nobody wrote down

  2. 54
    Anything it reads can give it orders

    Your instructions and a page the model was asked to read arrive as the same kind of thing in the same sequence, with nothing in the machinery marking which is which, so the only defence that holds is limiting what the system can do, rather than what you ask it to do.

    Read first: Nothing it writes ever runs itself, The step nobody wrote down, The refusal is written by the same hand

15 Small enough, fast enough, cheap enough

  1. 55
    The expensive way to do something easy

    On a narrow job a small model you run yourself is usually not a compromise but the right tool — the data never leaves the building, the answer arrives fast enough to sit inside other software, and the bill is a fraction of what a giant charges for the same easy work.

    Read first: Knowing how good it would be before building it

    Related: One right answer teaches almost nothing, Most of those digits were never doing anything

  2. 56
    One right answer teaches almost nothing

    A big model's answer is really a whole ranked row of scores, and training a small model on the entire row, near misses included, teaches it something a single correct label never could.

    Read first: The expensive way to do something easy, The only way to try harder is to write more

    Related: Most of those digits were never doing anything

  3. 57
    Most of those digits were never doing anything

    Storing each of a model's dials in fewer binary digits makes it far smaller and quicker for very little, right up until it doesn't, and what breaks first is never the everyday work anybody would think to check.

    Read first: The expensive way to do something easy

    Related: One right answer teaches almost nothing

  4. 58
    Reading is one sweep, writing is one piece at a time

    Everything you send is read in a single pass however long it is, and everything that comes back is produced one piece at a time with a full pass behind each one, which is why the answer is nearly all of the wait, why asking for brevity is a real saving, and why the same standing instructions are charged again on every call unless you arrange otherwise.

    Read first: Every word is final the moment it is written, The skill is what you leave out

    Related: The expensive way to do something easy

16 The whole picture

  1. 59
    Nothing it cannot do is a surprise

    Every limit people complain about — the miscounted letters, the invented citation, the missing last month — falls out of a specific property of the machine you have already read about, which is what makes the list predictable rather than mysterious.

    Read first: Nothing in there ever checked

    Related: One move, and everything built to cover for it

  2. 60
    One move, and everything built to cover for it

    The whole subject is one operation repeated — make a guess, be told how wrong it was — and once training ends the second half of that pair is gone, which is what nearly everything else on this site was built to replace.

    Read first: Why rules run out, Nothing it cannot do is a surprise
