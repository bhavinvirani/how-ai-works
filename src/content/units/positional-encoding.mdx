Write a shopping list, cut it into strips, and drop them on the floor.

Nothing has happened. Eggs, bread, coffee, milk — gather the strips in whatever
arrangement you like and it is the same shopping. That list was never in an
order. It only looked like it was, because paper has a top.

Now do it to the directions somebody gave you to their house. Second left, past
the church, right at the bridge. Shuffle those and you do not arrive late. You
arrive somewhere else entirely.

A sentence is directions. The machinery that reads it treats it as shopping.

## Look through the mechanism for the word "before"

Here is the whole of attention again, in one breath. Each word makes a question,
a label and its contents. Every question is compared against every label, the
scores each word produces are squashed until its shares add up to one, and the
word then rebuilds itself out of the mixture of everybody's contents.

Read that again and look for anything about order. It is not there. A score is
worked out from what two words mean — two words, not two places — and a heap of
scores between pairs is a heap, with no first and no last in it.

So these two arrive as the same thing:

_The student carried the box because she was strong_

_she box The strong carried student was the because_

To you, one of those is a sentence and the other is what happens when you drop
it. To attention they are indistinguishable, and not in a woolly way. Every word
in the scramble works out exactly the meaning it worked out in the sentence: the
same numbers, to the last decimal place, with nothing left over anywhere.

That is worse than it sounds, because order is not a decorative feature of
language. A great deal of the meaning lives in it. Who did what to whom is
order. Whether a _not_ reaches the end of a clause is order. A ticket from
London to Paris is a different ticket from one from Paris to London, and the
words printed on the two of them are identical.

This is also not an oversight that slipped past somebody. It is the bill for the
thing that made attention worth having. The machines before it read strictly
left to right and got order for nothing, precisely because they could only ever
be in one place at a time — which is the same reason they were slow and the same
reason they forgot the beginnings of long sentences. Attention looks everywhere
at once. It bought speed and reach by giving up the one thing that reading in
order had been carrying for free.

## The repair is an addition, and it goes in first

Whatever a word looks like on the page, inside the model it is a row of numbers
and nothing else. That row is the only thing attention ever gets to see.

Which means there is nowhere else to put the order. No second channel, no
sidecar, no small tag pinned to each word saying "this one was fifth". If
position is going to reach the comparisons, it has to travel inside the numbers,
because the numbers are all there is.

So that is what happens. Before any comparing starts, each word's row has a
second row added to it — a stamp that depends on nothing whatsoever except which
slot the word is sitting in. Slot one gets one stamp, slot two gets another, and
slot five gets the same stamp in every sentence anybody ever feeds the model.
That is **positional encoding**, and with the name taken off it is one addition.

The consequence is the whole point. _Box_ sitting fifth and _box_ sitting second
are no longer the same input. They are two different rows of numbers, so every
score either of them takes part in comes out different, and the heap of
comparisons is no longer the same heap.

Notice where in the order of events it happens. Before, not after. A note about
word order handed to the model at the end would be worthless, because by then
every comparison has already been made and the answers are already mixed. The
stamp goes on first exactly so that it can get into all of them.

Two things make a stamp a good one, and neither is obvious.

**Nearby slots should get similar stamps.** If slot eight and slot nine were
unrelated codes, then "two words apart" would be something a model could only
learn by memorising every pair of slots separately. Making neighbouring stamps
resemble each other turns the gap between two words into something a score can
feel directly.

**It should keep working past the lengths the model was trained on.** A model
trained on paragraphs gets handed whole documents by real users. A scheme that
simply runs out — nothing sensible for slot nine thousand, because it never met
one — fails at the point people care about most.

## Shuffle it yourself

Below is that nine-word sentence, twice. The top row is the sentence. The bottom
row is whatever arrangement you have asked for, run through the same machinery,
and the table underneath shows what the word you are watching comes out meaning
in each.

It opens on _she_, under the reading that hunts for what a word stands in for,
spending forty-three per cent of everything it has on _student_ — the number you
already met, and the reason any of this is worth building.

Now switch **Shuffle the words** on and watch that forty-three. It does not
vanish and it does not shrink. It slides along the row and lands back on
_student_, wherever the shuffle happened to throw it, and every other share
travels the same way, glued to its own word.

Then read the table. Four numbers in the left column, four in the right: 0.0576,
0.4321, 0.2943, 0.0576, in both. Not close. Identical, digit for digit, because
there was never anything in the arithmetic for the shuffle to disturb.

Switch **Stamp each word with the slot it sits in** on and the two columns come
apart. The row counting how much of a person _she_ is now carrying reads 0.3457
in the sentence and 0.3679 in the shuffle. Same words, same reading, same
machine. The only thing that changed is that every word now arrives knowing
where it is.

The fourth reading is where this stops being arguable. Switch the stamp back off
and choose **the word just before**. It is dead. Whichever word you pick spreads
its attention evenly over all nine — eleven per cent each, in both rows, every
time. That is not a broken head, and this time it is not an empty word either:
it is a question the machinery physically cannot answer, because nothing about
what two words mean can tell you which of them came first.

Now put the stamp back on and choose _box_. The row snaps into focus. _Box_
gives ninety-one per cent to _the_, the word immediately in front of it in the
sentence — and in the shuffle, where _box_ is sitting second, it gives
ninety-two per cent to _she_, the word immediately in front of it there. Same
reading, same word, a different neighbour, because it is now a different
arrangement.

A whole way of reading the sentence exists only because the stamp is there. That
is the clearest evidence available that position is load-bearing rather than
decorative.

One reminder, briefly, since it applies to every panel in this Part. The
arithmetic is the real mechanism. What is staged is where the scores come from:
a real model learns them, and what any one of its heads ends up following is
nobody's decision and mostly nobody's guess. These four are hand-written so that
nine words are enough to see a pattern.

<Aside title="Sine waves, and what most models do now">
  The 2017 design built its stamps out of sine and cosine waves running at many
  different speeds — a slow one that barely changes between neighbouring slots,
  a fast one that separates them cleanly, and every rate in between. It was
  chosen for exactly the two properties above, and it is where the sine and
  cosine you may have seen attached to this topic come from.

Most current large models have moved on. They arrange for the _score_ to depend
on the gap between two words rather than on the two slot numbers themselves, so
a model that has learned what "four words apart" feels like carries that to slot
nine thousand unchanged. The switch in the panel is this second kind, because it
is easier to watch. Both land before the comparison is finished, and that is the
part that never varies.

</Aside>

## Where it actually sits

In a working model this is one step, run once, right at the bottom. The words
become rows of numbers, the position stamps are added, and everything above —
every attention layer, all the dozens of them stacked up — inherits the result.
It is never repeated and never re-derived.

It is also one of the things people compare between models. When a new model
reads several times as much text at once as the one before it, the position
scheme is usually part of what changed, alongside the arithmetic that makes long
inputs expensive in the first place. It is the piece that decides whether slot
nine thousand means anything at all.

<DevAside>
  <Fragment slot="java">
    ```java
    float[] x = embedding(tokenIds[i]);   // what the word means
    float[] p = positionStamp(i);         // where it sits, and only that

    for (int d = 0; d < x.length; d++) {
      x[d] += p[d];                       // the whole of it
    }
    ```

    Two things worth noticing. `positionStamp` takes `i` and nothing else — not
    the word, not the sentence — which is why slot five is the same stamp
    forever. And it is a plain element-wise add into the array the meaning is
    already in, rather than a second array passed alongside, because there is no
    second array for attention to look at.

  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="python">
    ```python
    x = embedding(token_ids)                 # (positions, width)
    x = x + position_stamp(len(token_ids))   # same shape, added on
    ```

    Two lines, and the shapes are the tell: the stamp is built out of the
    sequence length alone and added straight onto the meanings. Delete the second
    line and nothing crashes. Training runs, the loss comes down, and you get
    fluent sentences that are unreliable about which noun did the verb — which
    can look like a general quality problem for weeks.

  </Fragment>
</DevAside>

<Checkpoint question="A model that translates well has its position stamps disabled by accident. It carries on training without a single error, and still produces fluent, grammatical-looking output. Why does nothing break — and what kind of mistakes would you expect to start seeing?">
  Nothing breaks because nothing was ever checking. Attention scores pairs of
  words by what they mean, and that calculation is perfectly well defined
  whatever order the words arrived in. Position was something the model was
  handed, not something the arithmetic required.

What survives is everything that does not depend on order: which words belong to
the same subject, which ones keep company, roughly what a passage is about. So
the output goes on looking like language, because most of what makes text look
like language is exactly that.

What goes is everything order was carrying. Who did what to whom. Which noun a
_not_ applies to. Directions, dates and comparisons — "London to Paris" and
"Paris to London" collapse into the same sentence, and so do "before" and
"after".

That is the shape of the failure worth remembering, because it is the expensive
kind. A crash is found the same afternoon. Output that reads perfectly and
quietly reverses one sentence in fifty is found by a customer.

</Checkpoint>
