Every word asks the whole sentence at once

Halfway down the second page of a tenancy agreement:

The tenant may not sublet the property without written consent, and she must give thirty days’ notice before leaving.

She. You stopped for perhaps a quarter of a second, went back up the line, found the tenant, and carried on reading.

Now notice what you did not do. You did not start the sentence again from the top. You did not go rummaging in a summary of everything you had read so far, hoping the answer was still somewhere in it. You asked one question — who is she? — and one word answered it. Everything else in the sentence stayed exactly where it was.

A machine that reads one word at a time cannot work like that. It carries a single running summary and rewrites it at every step, so a word twenty places back is not something it can go and look at. It is something it had to have remembered, and by then the remembering has usually been written over.

The idea in this unit is almost rude in its simplicity. Stop making the words take turns. Let every word ask its own question of every other word, all at the same moment, and let each one rebuild its meaning out of whichever words answered. That is attention, and nearly every language model in use today is built out of it.

Three things every word has to be able to make

Picture a large room at a conference. Two hundred people, and everyone is wearing a badge with a few words on it about what they do.

You want to know something specific, so you say what you are after. You have no idea who can help. What you actually do is read badges — not faces, not histories, just the short line each person has chosen to advertise about themselves — and you steer towards the ones that look like a match. Then you listen to what those people tell you, which is a great deal more than what fitted on a badge.

Three separate things are in play there, and keeping them apart is most of this unit. What you asked for. What each person advertised. What each person actually had to say.

Attention gives every word all three. A word arrives inside the machine as a list of numbers standing for its meaning, and the model squeezes that same list three different ways to produce:

  • a question — what this word wants to know about the rest of the sentence
  • a label — what this word is advertising about itself
  • its contents — what it hands over to anything that picks it

All three are made from the same word, and none of them is the word. The label is short and public, built to be matched against; the contents are the substantial part, and nobody looks at them until the matching is over. That separation is not decoration. It is what lets a word advertise one thing and deliver another, exactly like a badge.

The formal names are query, key and value, and you will meet them written as Q, K and V in every paper and every codebase. Question, label, contents is what they mean.

Every question, against every label

Now the arithmetic, which is smaller than you would expect.

Take one word — say she — and its question. Compare that question against the label of every word in the sentence, its own included. Each comparison produces a single number saying how well that question and that label go together.

Nine words, so nine numbers for she. Then the same for every other word: nine questions, each meeting nine labels, eighty-one numbers in all. No word waits for any other to finish, because no word needs anything another word has not already got.

Those raw numbers then get divided by one fixed amount before anything else happens — the square root of how many numbers each question is made of. Nothing deep is going on there. A question built from sixty-four numbers produces larger comparisons than one built from four, purely because more things are being added up, and the division cancels that out so the next step behaves the same whatever size the model is.

A budget, not a set of dials

Scores on no particular scale are not usable, so they go through one more step. Softmax takes any row of numbers and turns it into a row of shares: all positive, the largest still the largest, and adding up to exactly one.

That last property is the most consequential fact in this unit, and it is easy to read straight past.

Every word gets one unit of attention. Not one unit per relationship — one unit, full stop, to be divided among the entire sentence. A word cannot decide that three other words are all extremely relevant and lean hard on all three for nothing. Leaning harder on one word means, as a matter of arithmetic, leaning less on everything else.

Attention is a budget, not a set of independent dials. Hold on to that: a surprising amount of what follows is about giving a model more budgets to spend rather than a larger one.

After that, the last step is the easy one. The word’s new meaning is the mix — a large share of one word’s contents, a smaller share of another’s, a scrap of each of the rest. The word has been rebuilt out of the sentence around it.

What this round of attention is asking

“she” leaned hardest on “student”, spending 43% of everything it had there. Whatever is left over is all the rest of the sentence gets. “she” now carries a person more strongly than anything else it holds, at 0.43.

It opens on she, asking who it stands for. Forty-three per cent of everything she has goes to student. Sixteen per cent goes to box, which is a reasonable wrong answer rather than a random one — a box is at least the kind of thing a pronoun could be standing for. The other seven words get about six per cent each, which is this arithmetic’s way of saying it found nothing there.

Look at where student sits. Five words earlier, with four words in between — and one of those in-between words, because, is pressed right up against she and receives exactly the same six per cent as The at the very start of the sentence and strong at the very end. Distance contributed nothing at all. It is not that distance was overcome; there is nowhere in the arithmetic for distance to enter.

Then read the strip underneath. she arrived carrying one thing — it is a pointing word, and that is the whole of it — and it leaves carrying 0.43 of a person and 0.29 of a thing. The pronoun has been resolved, and nothing anywhere in that process looked up a rule about pronouns.

Now choose because. Eleven per cent to every word in the sentence, itself included, and the strip reports that it now means the plain average of the whole sentence — which is a precise way of saying it learned nothing. That is not the instrument giving up. because carries none of the meaning these questions are asking about, so its question matches every label equally badly, and a row of equal scores comes out of softmax as a row of equal shares. Nobody assigned that flat row. It was computed, by exactly the arithmetic that produced the interesting one, and it is the best evidence on this page that none of these answers were written down in advance.

Finally, change the question at the bottom and watch the same nine words rearrange themselves. Ask who is doing this, and carried leans on student. Ask what a word is caught up in, and box leans on carried. The sentence did not change. The question did.

What is real here, and what is stage scenery

One thing has to be said plainly, and this is the unit that owes it.

The arithmetic in that panel is the real thing. A score for every ordered pair of words, divided by the square root of the width, pushed through softmax so each row sums to one, then spent on the other words’ contents — that is attention as it runs inside a production model, not an impression of it. Every number on this page came out of it.

What is staged is where the scores come from. In a real model nobody writes down that a pronoun should go looking for a person. The three squeezes that turn a word into a question, a label and its contents are made of numbers that are learned — nudged a few million times towards whatever made the model’s answers less wrong, exactly like every other number in the machine.

So nobody decides what a round of attention ends up following, and nobody knows in advance. Here those numbers are hand-written, so that nine words produce a pattern legible enough to point at. That is the entire difference, and it matters in one specific way: a real round of attention is usually not interpretable at all. Researchers do occasionally find one doing something nameable, and those get written up precisely because they are unusual. The honest picture is thousands of them all doing something real, and almost none of them explicable.

Take the mechanism from that panel. Do not take the tidiness.

Why this beat everything that came before

Distance stopped mattering. In a machine that reads one word at a time, a word twenty places back has been through twenty rounds of rewriting before you can ask about it, and what survives is a smear. Here she reached student in one comparison, and it would have reached just as directly across five hundred words. There is no fading, because there is nothing to fade through.

And it all happens at once. Reading in order is not only a design choice, it is a speed limit: step two cannot begin until step one has finished, so a thousand-word passage takes a thousand turns however much machinery you own. Attention has no turns. Every question meets every label in the same instant, which means the work splits cleanly across thousands of processors.

The second point is the less obvious one, and it is the one that changed the industry. Attention did not win by understanding a single sentence better — early on it often did not. It won because it could be trained on far more text in the same week. Almost everything about the last few years follows from that.

What it costs

Every word compares itself against every word, so nine words means eighty-one comparisons and a thousand words means a million. Double the length of the text and you roughly quadruple the work — and the memory, which is usually what runs out first. That is why a model has a limit on how much it will read at once, why that limit is quoted in the specifications like a headline figure, and why a large slice of current research is people trying to get most of attention’s behaviour for less than all of attention’s bill.

Read left to right, in the words this unit has been using. QQQ is every word’s question, stacked into one grid; KKK is every word’s label; VVV is every word’s contents. QK⊤QK^{\top}QK⊤ compares every question against every label in a single multiplication — the whole table of eighty-one scores. dk\sqrt{d_k}dk​​ is that fixed division, where dkd_kdk​ is how many numbers a question is made of. softmax\text{softmax}softmax turns each row of the table into shares adding to one. Multiplying by VVV spends those shares on the contents.

There is nothing in that line you have not already done by hand.

For developers
double[][] scores  = matmul(queries, transpose(keys));
scale(scores, 1.0 / Math.sqrt(headWidth));
double[][] weights = softmaxRows(scores);   // every row now sums to 1
double[][] output  = matmul(weights, values);

The method worth naming carefully is softmaxRows, not softmax. Attention softmaxes each row on its own — one budget per word — and a helper that normalises the whole grid at once compiles, runs, and produces a model that never learns anything. The shapes do not protect you here; both versions are the same square of doubles going in and coming out.

Checkpoint

A model reading a long report attaches the word she to the wrong person. A colleague suggests fixing it by making the model pay more attention to the right person. Setting aside whether that is possible, what does it cost — and where does the cost land?

Show answerHide answer

It costs attention taken from every other word in that sentence. Each word has one unit to spend and no more, so raising the share on the right person necessarily lowers the share on everything else, including words the model was relying on. It is a trade, never an addition.

Worth keeping, because it generalises well beyond this unit. Any time somebody says a model should focus more on something, the honest version of that sentence has a second half — and less on something else. There is no setting anywhere in the machine that adds attention.
